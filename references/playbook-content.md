# Content playbook

Complete instructions for writing copy and articles, so no other writing skill is needed.

## Before writing

Take these from `SITE.md`, inferring what you can and logging assumptions under Decisions:

- Page type, primary keyword and the one action the page should drive.
- Reader: their problem, what they've tried, their objections and their own words.
- Offer: what's different from alternatives, and what proof exists.
- Awareness: does the reader know the problem, the solution type, or this product?

Search traffic usually knows the problem already, so get to the answer fast.

## Core rules

- Clarity over cleverness, specifics over adjectives, and honesty over hype.
- Connect each feature to a benefit and an outcome.
- Put one idea in each section.
- Use the reader's words. Pull real phrasing from forums and reviews.
- Prefer short words, active voice and direct statements.
- Aim for a reading grade of about 8 to 10, with paragraphs of 2 to 4 lines.
- Write three headline options and pick the clearest. Keep the alternatives in `SITE.md`.

## Landing page

Use the sections that fit, in this order:

1. **Hero:** specific outcome headline, one or two supporting sentences, and a CTA naming what the visitor gets.
2. **Answer line:** one 25 to 40 word sentence saying what this is and who it's for.
3. **Problem:** the reader's situation, with no product mention yet.
4. **Solution:** how this solves it.
5. **Benefits:** three to five at most, each with a feature behind it.
6. **Proof:** real numbers, reviews or logos. Skip if there are none.
7. **How it works:** three or four steps.
8. **Comparison:** honest, including at least one place an alternative wins.
9. **FAQ:** real objections, with answers under 50 words.
10. **Final CTA:** restate the outcome and remove risk.

## Article

1. Read the top results, and decide what you'll add that they lack.
2. Answer the search query in the first two sentences.
3. Outline `h2`s that follow the reader's next questions.
4. Add evidence to each section: an example, data, a step, a screenshot or a tested result.
5. Link to primary sources and related pages.
6. End when the question is answered, with a relevant next step.

Let the question set the length. Use a real author and only claims that person could stand behind.

## Trust signals

- **Experience:** what was tried, measured or observed.
- **Expertise:** accurate detail and correct terms, explained simply.
- **Authority:** real credentials and sources.
- **Trust:** dates, corrections, contact details, and honest limits.

Experience can't be invented. If nobody tested something, don't write as if they did.

## Conversion check

Score each key page out of 100:

| Factor | Points | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| Value clarity | 25 | Visitors know what it is within 5 seconds |
| Goal focus | 20 | One primary action, with a clear hierarchy |
| Message match | 15 | The page delivers what the search or link promised |
| Trust | 15 | Claims are backed up near the decision point |
| Friction | 15 | Fast on mobile, with a clear next step |
| Objections | 10 | "Will this work for me?" is answered |

Fix anything limiting the score before calling a page done. Below 70, fix the page before any A/B testing.

## AI writing tells

Replace these on sight:

| Replace | With |
|---|---|
| leverage, utilize | use |
| robust | reliable, or a specific strength |
| seamless | say what actually happens |
| streamline | simplify, or name the step removed |
| empower, enable | let, help |
| cutting-edge, next-gen | say what's new about it |
| delve into | look at |
| landscape, realm | market, field |
| journey | process, or cut it |
| pivotal, crucial | important, or show why |
| comprehensive | complete, or list what's covered |
| foster | build, encourage |
| moreover, furthermore | also, or cut it |
| in order to | to |
| serves as, stands as | is |

Rewrite these patterns:

- Openers like "In today's fast-paced world" or "Whether you're a...". Start with the point.
- "It's not just X, it's Y". State Y.
- Automatic groups of three, repeated rhythm, and matching sentence lengths.
- Em dashes used as a habit, bold scattered through text, and emoji headers.
- "Experts say" or "studies show" with no source. Name it or cut it.
- Inflated significance, such as "a testament to" or "plays a vital role".
- Endings like "In conclusion" or "the future looks bright".
- Hedging such as "it's important to note" or "generally speaking".
- Headings that repeat the sentence below them.
- Chat leftovers such as "Certainly!" or "I hope this helps".

## Editing pass

1. Read it aloud and fix anything a person wouldn't say.
2. Cut the first paragraph if the page still works without it.
3. Replace every vague claim with a specific one, or delete it.
4. Check each fact against its source.
5. Scan for the tells above, then reread the rewrite for new ones.
